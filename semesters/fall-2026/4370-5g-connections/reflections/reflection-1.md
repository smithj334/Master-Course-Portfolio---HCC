# Reflection 1 — How My Understanding of Telecommunications Evolved

When I started this course, my understanding of telecommunications was simple: you pick up your phone, it works, and you don't think about why. I knew the words — 5G, Wi-Fi, signal — but I didn't know what actually happened between a message leaving my phone and arriving somewhere else.

The first thing that changed was learning that everything starts with a signal. A call, a video, a text — it's all a signal carrying information, and the way that signal is built decides how well it survives the trip. That sounds basic, but once I saw it, I couldn't unsee it. Every "fast" or "slow" network I use now has a real reason behind it.

Then the picture got bigger. I learned how networks are actually put together — the pieces between a device and the internet, how wireless links behave over distance, and how one weak link can slow the whole chain down. That idea came back later in my midterm simulation, where packets queued up behind the slowest router and the whole trip got longer because of it. The theory and the simulation were saying the same thing.

The biggest shift came when AI entered the picture. At first, AI in telecom sounded like a buzzword to me. Then I built the models myself. I simulated packets moving through routers and measured the delay. I built agents that moved off a congested router on their own until the load balanced out. I trained a model to predict latency and watched it choose the faster route — stay put when the network was quiet, reroute when it was busy. AI stopped being a buzzword when I saw it make a real decision with real numbers.

By the end of the course I was looking at 6G, where AI isn't added on top of the network — it's built into the network from the start. That's a long way from where I began. I went from "my phone just works" to understanding the signal, the architecture, the queueing, the agents, and the predictions that make it work.

Now when I hear about 5G or 6G, I don't just hear a marketing word. I picture the pieces. I know there's a signal, a set of routers, traffic that piles up, and decisions being made about where it all goes. That understanding is the real thing I take out of this course.
